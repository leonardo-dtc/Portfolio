I read the play early, then commit.

What connects the rink, the lab, the rehearsal room and the projects with friends.

I was born in Beijing and grew up in Ontario and then South Florida, in a house with a Chinese mother and a Brazilian father where three languages were spoken. I came away with English, Mandarin and Spanish; Portuguese never took. Since 2025 I have boarded at Groton School in Massachusetts, where my week moves between the rink, the classroom and rehearsal.

What connects those three places is that each asks for the same habit: read early, then commit. In goal I am not the biggest goaltender on the ice, so I rely on reading the play, and on positioning, reflexes and skating rather than size. The best part of the net is the save, and then the quiet after it, when the only job was to stop the puck. On the viola, the part I like is being inside a story and telling it from my own perspective. In research, the question I keep coming back to is how the body fails and how it recovers, and what a treatment does to a body once it leaves the controlled setting.

Two projects came from that question. The aducanumab paper started at home, with my grandfather, who died of Alzheimer’s disease. It is a pharmacovigilance analysis of every adverse-event report filed for the drug, not an efficacy study, and I did it as independent work with an advisor at Harvard. The knee exoskeleton started on the ice and the pitch: I play three sports that all load the legs, ACL tears are common in every one of them, and rehabilitation is the part I wanted to work on. Biomedical engineering is where those two roads meet, and it is what I plan to study.

Most of what I make outside class is shared. OCAPEX began in 2022 after a visit to a senior living community that my viola teacher arranged, when the residents got up out of their chairs. It is now a registered nonprofit with more than seventy student musicians, and running it is mostly planning, teaching and showing up: booking the facilities, coordinating performers, making the programs. FreeCode Juniors is the same instinct pointed at coding, free Scratch and Python classes for kids who do not have access to them. Loquar and Daedalus are the projects with friends, a language app and a game, and both are still being built.

What I try to bring to a team is reliability and a short memory. I fight until the play is over. I forget a bad goal or a bad shift by the next whistle and own it after the game, and I would rather lift a teammate than make an excuse. In class I would rather put an idea on the table and have it argued with than keep it safe. I am willing to put in the work in the morning, during the day and at night, and since leaving home I have learned to manage my time and my stress, to be more independent, and to keep better habits.

Free time is my game with friends, online or in person, and a movie or a show while I eat. Mandarin lives at home, in Loquar, and now and then with friends as a secret language.
